# Slice S-003: When someone runs `bash scripts/slug.sh` with `SLUG_CUT=1` and a title whose slug is over 20 characters, they see the slug cut at a word boundary to at most 20 characters.

Feature: [brief](brief.md)
Slice: 3 of slug

## Outcome
When someone runs `bash scripts/slug.sh` with `SLUG_CUT=1` and a title whose
slug is over 20 characters, they see the slug cut at the last word boundary
at or before 20 characters, instead of a slug of any length.

## Rules

AC-12 Given `SLUG_CUT=1` and the title
      `Ten Tips for Writing Better Shell Scripts` (a 41-character slug),
      when someone runs the command with it,
      then it prints `ten-tips-for-writing` and exits 0.

AC-13 Given `SLUG_CUT=1` and the title `How to Name Files Well Today`
      (a 28-character slug whose 20th character is inside a word),
      when someone runs the command with it,
      then it prints `how-to-name-files` and exits 0: the word cut through
      is dropped whole.

AC-14 Given `SLUG_CUT=1` and the title
      `Supercalifragilisticexpialidocious Day` (its first word alone is
      over 20 characters),
      when someone runs the command with it,
      then it prints `supercalifragilistic`, the first 20 characters, and
      exits 0.

AC-15 Given `SLUG_CUT` unset, or set to anything but `1`,
      when someone runs the command with
      `Ten Tips for Writing Better Shell Scripts`,
      then it prints the whole slug
      `ten-tips-for-writing-better-shell-scripts` and exits 0, as before
      this slice.

AC-16 Given `SLUG_CUT=1` and a title whose slug is 20 characters or
      fewer (`Ten Tips for Writing`),
      when someone runs the command with it,
      then it prints the slug unchanged (`ten-tips-for-writing`) and exits 0.

## Out of scope
Switching the cut on for everyone and removing `SLUG_CUT` (slice 4).

## Touch points
`scripts/slug.sh`.
